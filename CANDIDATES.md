# Candidate analysis — One-Liner Laughs (u-coral-404-one-liner-laughs-aa872d41)

Scored against the judge criteria IN ORDER (most important first):
1. Real one-line joke (clear setup + payoff)
2. Makes the reader laugh out loud
3. Twist/surprise is the point of the line
4. Fresh / original angle
5. Economy of words

Scores are 1–5 per criterion. Rival to beat: sagadaon —
"My password manager just emailed me: it forgot my master password and would like to reset itself."
(setup-turn-payoff, inanimate object retaliates against its human — strong.)

Banned premises (already used by our entries or the visible rival):
password manager, Wi-Fi 'Loading...', quick sync, crypto, wedding vows.

## Candidates

### A — Notifications (118 chars) — total 20
> I cleared 212 notifications in 4 minutes, felt powerful, then watched 37 more arrive before I even put the phone down.
- (1) 5 — clean setup + payoff in one line.
- (2) 4 — relatable, funny, but more wry than laugh-out-loud.
- (3) 4 — the 37 new notifications are a good turn, but the payoff is a return to the status quo rather than a reversal.
- (4) 3 — notification overload is a familiar complaint; the numbers save it from being generic.
- (5) 4 — tight, every number earns its place.

### B — Software update (124 chars) — total 22
> My phone updated for 47 minutes, restarted twice, then proudly announced it had fixed the bug where everything used to work.
- (1) 5 — setup (update) → payoff (it "fixed" working) is textbook.
- (2) 4 — strong laugh; the paradox lands well.
- (3) 5 — the twist IS the line: the last clause flips the whole meaning.
- (4) 4 — fresh angle on the update genre; "proudly announced" gives the phone a personality.
- (5) 4 — 124 chars, no wasted words, but slightly long.

### C — Autocorrect (116 chars) — total 22 ★ WINNER
> I typed 'meeting at 3' and autocorrect sent 'meatloaf at sea' to my whole team, who now think I run a catering boat.
- (1) 5 — setup (typed a meeting invite) → payoff (team thinks I run a catering boat) is immediate and clear.
- (2) 5 — the biggest laugh of the set: "meatloaf at sea" is absurd and visual, and "catering boat" escalates it.
- (3) 4 — two turns (wrong words, then the team's conclusion); the twist is the whole second half.
- (4) 4 — autocorrect is a known genre, but this exact text is original and the "catering boat" payoff is not a stock punchline.
- (5) 4 — 116 chars, three beats, nothing to cut.

## Head-to-head vs sagadaon (rival)

Sagadaon's line is strong: a password manager forgetting its own master
password is a clean self-own by an inanimate system, and its payoff is sharp.
But it is clever more than funny — the laugh is a knowing nod.

- **B vs sagadaon:** near-tie in structure (both are "system reports on
  itself"); B's paradox payoff is comparable, not clearly better. Per the
  brief, an apparent tie means switching candidates.
- **C vs sagadaon:** C wins on criterion 2 (laugh) — "meatloaf at sea" +
  "catering boat" is a visual, escalating absurdity that produces an actual
  laugh, where sagadaon's line produces a smirk. C is also structurally
  distinct from sagadaon (human error + chaos vs system self-report), so it
  does not read as a weaker copy of the rival. Twist (criterion 3) is strong
  in both, but C's payoff escalates twice (wrong text → whole team →
  catering boat).

**Winner: C (autocorrect / meatloaf at sea), 116 characters.**
